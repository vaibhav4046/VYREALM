## Tagline

A complete video studio that runs entirely on your own machine. No cloud, no API key, no account.

## Elevator pitch (Devpost "short description", ~200 chars)

VYREALM turns a written brief into a rendered, narrated, captioned and thumbnailed video — then uploads it to YouTube. Every model runs locally on a 6 GB consumer GPU. Nothing leaves the machine.

---

## Inspiration

Every AI video tool is a rented API. You send your footage to someone else's GPU, you pay per second, and you cannot see what actually produced the frames. That is fine until you are a creator on a laptop, working offline, or handling material you are not allowed to upload.

We wanted the opposite: the whole pipeline on one ordinary Windows machine, and a receipt for every frame.

## What it does

One brief becomes a finished, publishable video:

1. **Plan** — the brief becomes a shot plan against a library of 36 formats across 5 delivery platforms.
2. **Render** — FFmpeg assembles the sequence at 1080p in landscape, portrait or square.
3. **Narrate** — Piper generates the voiceover locally on CPU.
4. **Caption** — faster-whisper transcribes that narration locally and returns timed captions marked `review_required`, because a draft transcriber should not be trusted verbatim.
5. **Score** — a sound bed is synthesised locally (ACE-Step) and mixed as editable layers.
6. **Thumbnail** — a 4K thumbnail is generated alongside the film, not after it.
7. **Publish** — the video and its matching thumbnail upload to YouTube as a private video.

Generation is real: LTX-Video 2B and Wan2.2-TI2V-5B run under a local ComfyUI on an RTX 3050 with 6 GB of VRAM.

## The part we are proudest of: it refuses to lie about what it made

Most of the engineering here is a provenance system that constrains the product rather than flattering it:

- `runtime/generation-gate.mjs` refuses to label composited footage as generated.
- `runtime/format-render.mjs` stamps `sourceMethod: 'composited-from-existing-footage'` on every batch output.
- `scripts/render-format-batch.mjs` writes `"No pixels were generated by this batch."` into its own evidence file.
- Every generated still lands at `review_required` and needs a human to inspect it and write defect notes before it can become a shot.
- `assertNotBulkPublishable` blocks mass-uploading recut variants, because that would breach YouTube's policy on repetitive automated content.

We shipped a tool that tells on itself. See [SHOWCASE.md](SHOWCASE.md), where we state that the 151 rendered variants come from about 91 seconds of source footage, and curate the showcase down to 19 files rather than pad it.

## How we built it

- **Runtime**: Node.js 24, `node:sqlite`, zero npm dependencies to boot the studio.
- **Video**: FFmpeg, with a quality-detector suite (temporal flicker, motion stall, detail collapse, morphing geometry, colour drift) calibrated against measured clips.
- **Models**: LTX-Video 2B distilled Q8 GGUF, Wan2.2-TI2V-5B Q4_K_M GGUF, Piper `en_US-ljspeech-high`, faster-whisper `tiny.en`, ACE-Step — all local, all pinned by hash.
- **Interface**: a browser studio plus an Electron desktop build and an MCP server, so agents can drive the same engine.

## Challenges

- **6 GB of VRAM.** Wan2.2 peaks at 5.85 GiB. Every choice downstream is shaped by that 0.15 GiB of headroom.
- **Honest provenance is harder than faking it.** The review gate makes the product slower on purpose.
- **Draft ASR.** `tiny.en` transcribed "Someone" as "some more" in our own demo run. Rather than hide that, captions ship as reviewable drafts.

## What's next

- More original source footage — the current library is the binding constraint, not the pipeline.
- A larger local video model as 6 GB-class quantisations land.
- Native-resolution delivery instead of upscaling from 512×288–1024×576.

## Try it

```powershell
git clone https://github.com/vaibhav4046/VYREALM
cd VYREALM
node server.js
```

Then open http://127.0.0.1:4173. It boots with no npm install, no models, no GPU and no account. `npm test` needs FFmpeg in `workers/tools/` and tells you so up front.
