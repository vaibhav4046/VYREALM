# BETWEEN TWO ARMIES

Original 30-second Mahabharata teaser. Research date: 2026-09-07.

This is an original adaptation brief for VYREALM. It uses the user's reference as a requested quality category, not as footage, dialogue, music, character design or a shot-for-shot template. No comparison with the reference is claimed by this document.

## What the story is about

At Kurukshetra, Arjuna's distress comes from seeing his own relatives and teachers among the people he may kill. His response is moral anguish, not simply fear of losing. Krishna, serving as his charioteer, becomes his teacher. The teaser moves from an immense public conflict to an intimate decision. It ends in attentive resolve, before showing combat.

## Original editorial baseline

> At Kurukshetra, two armies face the dawn. Arjuna sees the men who raised him, waiting beyond his bow. His hand falters. Beside him, Krishna counsels action without attachment to reward. Before he can fight, a warrior must confront his deepest doubt. Duty. Compassion. Consequence. Between two armies, the struggle begins within.

This narration is original poetic exposition, not a quotation from the Mahabharata or a translation of the Bhagavad Gita. ## Selected narration after local-model pass and editorial review

> At Kurukshetra, Arjuna asks Krishna to halt their chariot. Across the field stand his kin and teachers. His breath catches. His bow slips. Krishna counsels action without attachment to reward. The weight of kinship remains. Arjuna lifts his gaze, listening. Between the armies, he must understand what duty demands.

## Six shots, five seconds each

1. **The field:** a low lateral camera passes a textured wooden chariot wheel; dust drifts; white horse shoulder and cloth standard in midground; the opposing army is far beyond. Warm dawn, no city, mountains or fantasy fortress.
2. **The cost:** close portrait of Arjuna on the chariot. His gaze finds familiar people offscreen; eyes moisten, a breath catches; only a small camera move. Wind stirs two strands of hair and his saffron shoulder cloth.
3. **The hand:** insert of the same bracer and hand loosening around Gandiva's wrapped grip. The bow lowers a few centimetres toward the chariot floor. No drawn arrow, firing, impossible bow deformation or hand multiplication.
4. **The guide:** intimate three-quarter Krishna portrait with a rein in hand. He looks toward Arjuna with steady compassion. His yellow cloth and restrained crown details move in the breeze. No lip-sync claim; narration stays offscreen.
5. **The listening:** over Krishna's shoulder toward Arjuna, preserving their eyeline and the light direction. Arjuna steadies his breathing and lifts his eyes. Reuse the same face reference; Krishna's face is not separately regenerated in this shot.
6. **The choice:** a closer Arjuna profile, turning only slightly toward the field; jaw relaxes into resolve. A narrow rim of dawn light meets his cheek; distant flags move. The original title appears as an editorial overlay at 28–30 seconds, never as text generated into the image.

## Costume and continuity decisions

The surviving literary sources do not establish one photographically verifiable costume or the precise archaeological date of these visual details. Treat costume as a respectful epic interpretation, not historical proof.

- Arjuna: one original adult South Asian face, medium-brown skin, brown eyes, long black hair tied back, clean-shaven, a narrow gold headband; textured ochre wrap, saffron upper cloth, restrained fitted protective metalwork, simple arm guards. Keep exact face, hairline, cloth placement, jewellery and right-hand bracer across shots.
- Krishna: one original adult South Asian face with conventionally blue-toned dark complexion, black curls, yellow pitambara, restrained gold ornaments and a small peacock-feather crown element. These are familiar devotional visual conventions, not claims drawn from the few selected verses. He is the charioteer and guide, with reins rather than a sword in his hands.
- Setting: dry north Indian plain, textured earth, wood-and-metal chariot, linen/cotton-like banners, hazy opposing ranks. Background figures should be sparse and distant enough for the local model to render coherently.
- Exclude European plate armour, Roman helmets, fantasy horns, modern guns, medieval castles, neon magic circles, sci-fi vehicles, generic Sanskrit-looking gibberish, unexplained explosions and floating weapons.
- Same dawn direction in every shot: warm key from camera-left and cool ambient fill; subdued saffron, brown, brass and deep blue. Do not change time of day or costume between shots.

## Sound and performance

Use locally generated or clearly licensed wind, horse tack, fabric, restrained low drum and conch-like layers. Label synthesized sound honestly; do not claim a recorded conch if it is an oscillator. Wind establishes space, leather/cloth follows the hand insert, the music drops under Krishna's appearance, then one low impact punctuates Arjuna's final gaze. Avoid incessant trailer booms and distorted clipping. Narration is intelligible and centered; sound layers support the moral pause. Do not use devotional chanting or verses merely as generic spectacle.
